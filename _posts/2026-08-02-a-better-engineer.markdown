What exactly does it mean to be a good engineer? 

I was recently given a task that I had no prior exposure to. It wasn't hard, and honestly it wasn't all that complex either. I knew what I was building, I just didn't know how to do it. My understanding of a good engineer, at the time, was someone who was good at solving problems. Unknowingly, I attached the idea of being a good problem solver to the idea of not needing help. And so that's what I aimed for.

I thought through the problem, and still, I had no idea how to solve it. Eventually, I was given some direction and ended up solving it. But I was bothered. What, in retrospect, seemed so simple took me (what I believed to be) far too long to figure out, and worst of all, I couldn't do this simple task myself.

Looking back, what actually slowed me down wasn't the task itself. It was how I imagined a good engineer would perform in that scenario.. someone who doesn't get stuck and doesn't need to be shown the way. So when I got stuck, I didn't experience it as learning something new. I experienced it as failling.

A few days later, I happened to run into a situation where I needed to implement that same task. And so again, I thought through the problem, but this time I was able to solve it.. immediately, and without help.

I'd love to frame that as proof that I finally figured it out on my own, but that's not what happened. I solved it the second time because someone had shown me the shape of it once already... that's kinda what direction is. It isn't a shortcut around competence but rather how competence gets built. I didn't become a better engineer by avoiding help. I'd become one by receiving it.

That's what I had backwards. I thought good engineers were the ones who never got stuck. They're actually the ones who keep getting stuck, and don't spend their energy protecting an image of themselves while they wait to figure it out on their own. After all, that's how they grow.

A *better engineer*, I'm starting to think, isn't someone who solves everything alone. It's someone who makes tomorrow easier for themselves, someone who's willing to look incompetent today so they're not stuck the same way again later.
